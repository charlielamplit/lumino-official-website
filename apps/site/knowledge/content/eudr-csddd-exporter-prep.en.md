# Exporter briefing: EUDR vs CSDDD — what to prepare first

- Type: regulations · practice
- Industry: wood / rubber / agri-commodities · manufacturing
- Service: export compliance
- Updated: 2026-11-02

## In one line

**EUDR** is about deforestation-free, plot-level traceability for listed commodities. **CSDDD** is about company due diligence across the value chain. Both can cascade to exporters — with different evidence packs.

## How they differ

| | EUDR | CSDDD |
|---|---|---|
| Core question | Is this shipment deforestation-free and traceable? | Does the company run due diligence on human rights and environmental impacts? |
| Typical focus | Listed commodities and derived products (official list prevails) | In-scope companies and their value chains (thresholds/timelines per final text) |
| What plants are asked for | Geo-location, legality, supply-chain map, mitigation | Policies, supplier code, risk assessments, grievance evidence |
| Link to carbon | Not a PCF rule — but buyers may ask in the same RFI | Climate may appear, yet scope is broader than product carbon |

## If you sit in an EUDR-relevant chain

1. Check whether the product (or derived path) is in scope
2. Request upstream: coordinates / production area, legal right, harvest period
3. Keep lot-level traceability into finished goods
4. Store exportable diligence support files per buyer template

## If the buyer pushes CSDDD-style diligence

1. Clarify questionnaire vs signed commitments + audits
2. Minimum pack: policy, key suppliers, risk results, remedy/grievance channel
3. Prioritise high-risk categories for document or site checks

## Four-week starter list

Map EU buyers and at-risk categories → tier-1 supplier geography → gap list for missing plot/legality evidence → one compliance spokesperson.

> Scope and dates follow official EU acts and guidance and may change. Practical orientation only — **not legal advice**.
