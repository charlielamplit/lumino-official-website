# CBAM definitive period: what importers ask for — and what plants should prepare first

- Type: regulation · practice
- Industry: steel / aluminium (and other covered goods)
- Service: CBAM
- Updated: 2026-10-30
- Readers: plant EHS, trade, group compliance coordinators

## In one line

In the EU, the **importer** usually files. Your plant’s job is to deliver product and installation data that is **clear, consistent, and evidence-backed**.

## Who does what

| Role | Main duty |
|---|---|
| EU importer / declarant | Files with the authority |
| Export plant / precursor supplier | Provides product, installation, energy and material data plus evidence |
| Service partner (e.g. Lumino) | Helps organise scope, collection, calculation and packs — **does not** replace the importer’s filing |

## Five things importers often request

1. **Product & codes** — name, CN/HS, quantity, period (use the buyer’s import classification)
2. **Installation & process** — which site, which line, which production route
3. **Activity data** — output, fuels, purchased power/heat, key precursors
4. **Emissions path** — actual vs default, with gaps and assumptions written down
5. **Evidence** — bills, statements, metering notes, file versions — not just screenshots

## Five steps for plants

1. List export products and the basis for codes
2. Sketch sites / installations and main lines
3. Pull recent monthly output, fuel and purchased energy ledgers
4. List precursor purchases and any supplier statements
5. Map the importer template, mark gaps and delivery dates

## What not to overthink first

- You do not need to memorise the full legal text up front
- Free allocation and origin carbon-price offsets belong in a project check under **current rules**
- If one shipment spans several codes, **calculate separately** — do not average

## Anchors

- Regulation (EU) 2023/956 and later amending / implementing acts (use the version for the reporting period)
- Exact fields follow the importer template and current implementing rules
